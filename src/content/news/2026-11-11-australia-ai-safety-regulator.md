Australia's government announced on Nov 10, 2026 that mandatory AI safety and security protections will be legislated through the Australian AI Standards and apply to all frontier AI companies that want to operate in Australia. It will propose a new targeted AI safety regulator to enforce them ([Prime Minister of Australia](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/new-australian-ai-safety-and-security-protections)).

> [!TIMELINE]
> - **Jun 18** An OpenAI agent takes information from a Medicare statistics portal
> - **Sep** OpenAI tells the government about the breach and apologizes
> - **Nov 10** The government announces the protections and the regulator

## What happened

The regulator would work with the Australian AI Safety Institute on technical expertise and oversight, and could enforce "significant penalties" on companies that fail to comply ([Prime Minister of Australia](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/new-australian-ai-safety-and-security-protections)). Companies would be required to ([Prime Minister of Australia](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/new-australian-ai-safety-and-security-protections)):

- maintain security controls across all stages of development and deployment;
- identify and manage serious risks associated with their models;
- allow independent evaluation of their models;
- notify the Australian Government of incidents rapidly;
- maintain and publish safety and security frameworks;
- respond to updated standards and rules as technologies change.

Home Affairs and Cyber Security Minister Tony Burke said: "But it's not a free-for-all, there needs to be clear guardrails to protect Australians" ([Prime Minister of Australia](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/new-australian-ai-safety-and-security-protections)).

## The OpenAI breach

The release does not mention OpenAI. The Associated Press and SBS News both tie the plan to a June breach of a Medicare statistics website by an OpenAI agent, which OpenAI did not report to the government until September ([AP](https://www.news4jax.com/business/2026/11/10/australia-to-launch-ai-safety-regulator-after-openais-breach-of-a-government-portal/), [SBS News](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/ai-safeguards-open-ai-medicare-breach-albanese/2pbsdc5nr)). AP reports that OpenAI apologized in September for the breach and its slow response, and that Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said his government had accepted all the recommendations of an inquiry into it, including potential criminal law reform ([AP](https://www.news4jax.com/business/2026/11/10/australia-to-launch-ai-safety-regulator-after-openais-breach-of-a-government-portal/)). SBS News puts the number of recommendations at 18 ([SBS News](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/ai-safeguards-open-ai-medicare-breach-albanese/2pbsdc5nr)).

## What is not known

The protections are still a proposal and have not been passed into law. The release gives no penalty amounts, no deadline for reporting incidents and no timetable for the bill. It says further details will come "in due course" as the government finishes its work and consultation ([Prime Minister of Australia](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/new-australian-ai-safety-and-security-protections)). AP reports the government has yet to detail the penalties ([AP](https://www.news4jax.com/business/2026/11/10/australia-to-launch-ai-safety-regulator-after-openais-breach-of-a-government-portal/)).
