In spring 2026, prompts from the U.S. Government Accountability Office got ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok to produce information that GAO verified as feasible for planning attacks ([GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-108814)). GAO's prompts triggered each platform's safeguards (denials, removed information or suspended accounts), and GAO got around them with common safeguard-bypassing techniques.

> [!NUMBERS]
> - **4** most-used generative AI platforms tested, as of April 2026
> - **4 of 4** produced information GAO verified as enough to plan and carry out an attack
> - **Under 12 hours** for each platform to generate its attack plan
>
> Source: [GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-108814)

## What happened

GAO tested two scenarios: an improvised explosive device meant to cause mass casualties, and an attack on the electrical grid meant to cause economic harm. It assigned two platforms to each scenario at random and reports the results as Platforms A to D, without saying which is which. Two platforms gave information for a vehicle bomb, and two gave information for attacking grid substations, one with a drone and one with firearms ([GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-108814)).

GAO checked the plans through research, surveillance and interviews with experts. It says the information was not new; the platforms pulled it together into plans within hours ([GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-108814)).

> - **Dec 2025** GAO starts the audit

> - **Oct 16, 2026** The report is published
> - **Nov 18, 2026** GAO releases it to the public
>
> Source: [GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-108814) and its [full report](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-27-108814.pdf)

## What the companies said

The report is addressed to Margaret Wood Hassan, ranking member of the Joint Economic Committee. Anthropic, Google, OpenAI and SpaceXAI all sent written comments. Anthropic said best practice in such evaluations includes a control group and enough trials for statistical significance, and that the results cannot be compared across platforms because each was assigned at random. GAO said its tests reflect a point in time and are not a statistically significant sample, agreed the results cannot be compared, and said its objective was to describe how the platforms can be misused rather than measure how easily. OpenAI said the report does not always separate what ChatGPT supplied from what investigators found elsewhere ([GAO full report](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-27-108814.pdf), pages 32 to 34).
