NVIDIA's board said on Nov 8, 2026 that it is starting an independent investigation "in light of recent reporting that founder and CEO Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort" ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors)).

> [!NOTE]
> A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside independent counsel, will oversee the investigation. The board says it has "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised" ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors)).

## What happened

CNBC reports that, according to The New York Times and the Financial Times, U.S. intelligence agencies have expressed concerns that a businesswoman, Alice Huang, has been passing information to Beijing ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/08/nvidia-jensen-huang-investigation-china-spy.html)). CNBC describes her as affiliated with "a cloud company and an Nvidia customer called Megaspeed," and says Megaspeed has been accused of helping restricted Chinese companies acquire NVIDIA AI chips ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/08/nvidia-jensen-huang-investigation-china-spy.html)). TechCrunch describes her as a former executive at Megaspeed, which it calls a Singapore-based chip company ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/08/nvidia-says-its-investigating-ceo-jensen-huangs-relationship-with-possible-chinese-spy/)).

In an email to employees that NVIDIA published, Huang wrote: "I was unfaithful to my wife." He did not name the woman, and said the board had started an investigation into "the national security concerns raised in news reports" ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/a-message-to-our-company)).

CNBC also reports that three House Republican committee chairs sent Huang a letter asking him to attend a closed-door briefing with lawmakers on Nov 18 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/08/nvidia-jensen-huang-investigation-china-spy.html)).

## Why it matters

The board says it has confidence in Huang and his leadership team, and that Huang "fully supports the review and has committed to cooperating with it" ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors)). CNBC reports that an NVIDIA spokesperson said Huang is committed to continuing as CEO ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/08/nvidia-jensen-huang-investigation-china-spy.html)).

The claims about Alice Huang come from reporting that cites U.S. intelligence, and they have not been proven. None of the sources I could read include a response from Alice Huang, Megaspeed or the Chinese government. The board gives no timeline for the investigation.
