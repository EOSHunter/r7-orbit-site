On Nov 25, 2026 the FCC's Space Bureau granted, in part, SpaceX's application for Starmind, up to 1,000,000 satellites that would run as data centers in orbit ([FCC](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-427572A1.pdf), [FCC order](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-1273A1.pdf)). The FCC calls it the first authorization in U.S. history for a constellation of data centers in space.

> [!NOTE]
> The grant covers radio links for backup and emergency telemetry, tracking and command (TT&C, the signals used to monitor and steer a satellite), in Ka-band frequencies used "only on an unprotected and non-interference basis". Starmind's main links are optical, to SpaceX's Starlink satellites ([FCC order](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-1273A1.pdf)).

## What happened

The order, DA 26-1273, places the satellites in shells 500 to 1,002 km up. It denies petitions from Kuiper Systems (Amazon Leo), Viasat and the American Astronomical Society. The 15-year license starts when SpaceX certifies that the first Starmind satellite is in orbit ([FCC order](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-1273A1.pdf)).

The order defers SpaceX's request to dispose of satellites above 600 km into Earth disposal orbits or heliocentric orbits instead of by atmospheric re-entry. SpaceX must file to modify the license before deploying satellites that will not re-enter ([FCC order](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-1273A1.pdf)).

> [!TIMELINE]
> - **Mar 6, 2026** Kuiper, Viasat and the AAS file petitions to deny
> - **Nov 25, 2026** The Space Bureau grants SpaceX's application in part
> - **Nov 25, 2033** Deadline for one satellite
> - **Nov 25, 2035** Deadline for 10% of the satellites
> - **Nov 25, 2038** Deadline for 50%
> - **Nov 25, 2040** Deadline for the rest
>
> Source: [FCC order](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-1273A1.pdf)

If SpaceX misses any deadline, the authorization becomes void ([FCC order](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-1273A1.pdf)).

## What the FCC left open

Comments questioned whether orbital data centers can work, how they shed heat, and whether they are less efficient than data centers on the ground. The order says these concerns "are not obviously related to the limited mandate given by Congress to the FCC to regulate radiocommunications" and declines to rule on them. It treats the astronomers' worry about infrared waste heat as outside its scope, and requires no further environmental review under the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) ([FCC order](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-1273A1.pdf)).

## Why it matters

Chairman Brendan Carr said "SpaceX and the many competitors working on their own orbital data centers are meeting the moment." The FCC says it is reviewing other orbital data center proposals ([FCC](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-427572A1.pdf)). Neither FCC document gives a launch date, cost, computing capacity or customer for Starmind.
